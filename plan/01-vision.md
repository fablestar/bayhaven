# 01 · Vision

## The pitch in one line

**Bayhaven** is a sunny, neon, open-world crime comedy that runs in a browser tab, built live in nine days by one AI.

The race line, for anything that talks about the project: *an AI game studio is trying to make GTA 6 before GTA 6. It won't be GTA 6. Watch it try, then play what it made today.* The plan names the game it races so every session knows the bar. The game itself never names it: the name check fails the build if it does.

## What the player does

You are Mags, a 61-year-old school bus driver who was the best getaway driver on the coast before that, and Teo, the 24-year-old small-time crook next door who streams everything to 212 followers. A developer is bulldozing your trailer park. You steal cars, outrun a sheriff who films his chases for a reality show, and pull one big job in the middle of a hurricane. The city is the star: drive anywhere, walk anywhere, cause trouble, and the whole town pulls out a phone and films you.

## Pillars

Every session and every cut decision is checked against these six.

| # | Pillar | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Alive in ten seconds** | Any ten seconds of play shows motion that isn't yours: traffic, people, palms in the wind, neon switching on, weather. A still city is a failed build. |
| 2 | **Driving first** | The car is the main verb. Handling has to feel good within 30 seconds on a keyboard, a phone and a gamepad. Everything else is built around the drive. |
| 3 | **Everyone is filming** | Our own idea, and the game's signature. Trouble draws a live audience. Police heat is measured in viewers, the HUD looks like a stream overlay, and you lose the cops by being boring until the viewers leave. |
| 4 | **Runs anywhere, right now** | A link, not an install. First frame in 3 seconds on a laptop, playable at 30 fps on a mid-range phone. No loading screen. |
| 5 | **A visible jump every day** | Each day's build has one headline you can see in a clip without reading anything. Old builds stay playable, so the jump can be checked. |
| 6 | **Code first** | The whole game runs from code alone: every model, texture, sound and song has a version generated by code the studio wrote. That is the floor, and it is also the look. On top of it, an art department of generative models can make hero assets from the studio's own requests: the leads, hero vehicles, landmarks, songs, spoken lines, billboard pictures. Take them away and the game still plays. |

## What a normal person on X should feel in a 10-second clip

They know nothing about the project. The clip autoplays with sound off. In order, they should think:

1. **"That looks nice."** (seconds 0–2) Pastel buildings, a huge sunset, neon, palm trees. It reads as "open-world game in a beach city" before any text.
2. **"Oh, something's happening."** (seconds 2–7) One clear action: a drift, a jump, a chase with a live chat going wild, a grandma honking a bus through traffic.
3. **"Wait, an AI made this yesterday? And I can play it?"** (seconds 7–10) The corner badge says `Day 5 build` and the caption carries the link.

Rules that follow from this:

- Every day's clip is shot at golden hour or at night with neon, never at flat noon.
- The day badge (`Day N build`) is always in the frame, bottom right, readable at phone size.
- One action per clip. No menus, no text walls, no tutorial prompts on screen.
- The stream-overlay HUD (pillar 3) makes any clip look like a stream. That is on purpose: it reads as native on X.

## What "GTA 6 level" means here

It means the **feeling of a big, dense, living city you can do anything in**. It does not mean photoreal. A browser game built by an AI in nine days can't match a thousand-person studio on fidelity, and trying would look worse than choosing a style. So every big-budget feature gets a version that fits the medium:

| The big-budget way | The Bayhaven way |
|---|---|
| Photoreal city | A stylised "postcard" city: flat-shaded low-poly, pastel walls, neon, a painted sky. Looks deliberate at any resolution. |
| Hand-built map | A procedural city (seeded, the same for everyone) with hand-written landmarks in every district. Size is cheap; landmarks are where the effort goes. |
| Motion-captured actors | Boxy "toy" people with canvas-drawn faces and procedural walk cycles. Charm instead of realism. |
| Licensed radio | Seven original stations. Songs are step-sequenced in code; DJ lines and ads are written text with a blip voice. |
| Thousands of scripted behaviours | Six pedestrian states that read clearly from a moving car: walk, hang out, flee, film you, fall over, get up. |
| Simulated physics | Arcade physics we wrote ourselves: flat ground, 2D bodies, generous drift. The coast is flat. That helps. |
| Voice-acted cutscenes | Puppet-show cutscenes: camera moves, subtitles, a speech blip per character. Skippable in one tap. |
| 100 hours of content | A 20-mission story, of which seven ship by day 9 at the middle compute level. The rest continue after release. |

## Scale targets

The same plan under three levels of compute, meaning how many sessions can run each day. Each column is what is playable in the day 9 build.

| | Low (1 session a day) | Mid (4 sessions a day) | High and Max (bonus pool) | The full vision (after release) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Map | The 3.2 km beachfront, about 1.4 km² | Full 6.4 × 4.8 km city, 10 districts, 1–3 landmarks each | Every district built to its bible file, three small interiors | Plus Marlin County: 12.8 × 9.6 km, seven more zones |
| Vehicles | 6 | 15 land, 3 water | 31 land, 3 water | 51 road vehicles, 10 boats, 8 aircraft |
| Traffic and people | Traffic; a handful of people | District mixes, 8 kinds of people | Plus wildlife, buses you can ride, a seven-day week | Plus the monorail, ferries, seasons |
| Police heat | Levels 1–3 | Levels 1–5, helicopter, roadblocks | Same | Plus boat and air units |
| Story missions | 2 | 6 (a full short arc with an ending) | All 20 of chapter 1 | 60 in three chapters |
| Side characters | 0 | 0 | 3 Regulars, 12 jobs | 12 Regulars, 46 jobs |
| Playable leads | 1 (Teo) | 2 with switching | 2 with switching | 2 with switching |
| Radio | None | 7 stations | 12 stations | 12 stations, 72 songs, 24 mixtapes |
| Side activities | 0 | 4 kinds, 65 things to find | 10 kinds, 6 small games, 20 races, 254 things to find | 20 jobs, 12 small games, 60 Local Man events, 284 things to find |
| Weather | Rain, storms, hurricane | Same | Same | Plus seasons |
| Clips | Scripted daily clip | Scripted daily clip, photo mode | A record button: 20-second clips saved from the game | Plus a gallery and shared race ghosts |
| Saving | Yes | Yes, carries between daily builds | Same | Same |

The low column is still a game you can show: a drivable beachfront with traffic, police, a mission and a storm. The plan never promises the last two columns in public copy. The last column is the whole game written out in `bible/`: it is the vision, with a tier on every item, not a date.

## Not in this game

Stated once so sessions don't drift into them:

- **No online multiplayer.** Single player only.
- **No building interiors**, apart from three small ones in the bonus pool. Shops open as menu overlays at the door.
- **No recorded actors.** Subtitles and speech blips, or synthetic stock voices when the art department is on. Never a copy of a real person's voice.
- **Nothing graphic in close-up, no slurs, nothing explicit.** It is a crime game for adults, in the register of its genre: lethal weapons, deaths, crews and police who shoot back, a criminal underworld with real stakes, swearing, dark jokes. It is drawn low-poly, so nothing is graphic in close-up. Out: slurs, jokes aimed at any group for who they are, explicit sexual content, sexual violence, harm to children, real people.
- **No real people, real brands, real places, and nothing another game owns.** Approved names are in `05-content.md`. The genre's conventions belong to nobody and are expected: a heat meter, stolen cars, chases, radio stations with DJs and fake ads, parody brands, heists, a sunny crime city, a crew of crooks. A stranger should know the genre in two seconds and find nothing copied.
- **Nothing about digital money.** No in-game item, place, brand or joke about it.
- **No photo-real claims.** The word "photoreal" never appears in copy.

## The title

The game is called **Bayhaven**, the same as the city. The wordmark is our own letterforms drawn in code (see `07-art-and-audio.md`). No subtitle. The day badge sits next to it: `Bayhaven · Day 4 build`.

## Tone

A crime game with a sense of humour, in the register of its genre: guns, chases, crews, deaths, swearing and dark jokes under a postcard sky. The satire is about homeowner associations, wellness influencers, luxury condo developers, reality-TV policing, billboard lawyers and the weather. It is never about real people or about any group of people for who they are. Mags and Teo like each other. The game likes them too.
