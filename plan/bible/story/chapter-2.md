# Bible · story · Chapter 2: Hurricane Season

The outline of the second chapter: the arc, eight new people and twenty missions, numbered 21 to 40. The storm is over, the bill has arrived, and a polite group of lenders is buying the damaged city one block at a time and burning what it cannot buy. Every mission runs on the systems in `04-systems.md` plus five small new ones, named below.

Tier: D, all of it.

## The arc

Three weeks after the hurricane. Bellweather was found in a gazebo, signed everything his lawyers put in front of him, and has not been seen since. His tower, his billboards and his debts have gone to the people who lent him the money: the **Seawall Group**, led by **Constance Vane**. Seawall is never rude. It sends muffin baskets. It also owns the insurer that is denying every claim in town, and it buys each wrecked block the week its owner gives up. Owners who do not give up have fires. Two are dead already, and the fire marshal, who has a new boat, called both electrical. Seawall's relief contractors wear polo shirts and carry rifles.

**Act 1 · The Bill (21 to 25).** Mags is asked back to drive Route 9, because half the bus fleet is in the bay. A city bus is invisible, which makes it the best cover she has ever had. Teo has two million followers, a statue and no idea what to do with either. They learn how Seawall works, and that the park's lane, gate and beach strip are pledged against one of Bellweather's loans. In the background, with the port leased to Seawall, somebody is moving pills through berth nine, and the relief depot's painkillers keep walking out.

**Act 2 · The Offer (26 to 30).** Constance offers to buy Pelican Rest and is charming about it. Teo announces a relief concert before he has booked anyone. Dale Rucker, his show cancelled, runs for mayor against Bunny Castellano on Seawall's money, with off-duty deputies as its muscle, and the town starts watching polls the way it used to watch chases.

**Act 3 · The Campaign (31 to 35).** The concert happens, in spite of a power cut. The small print comes out at a debate held in a drive-in. Forty forgotten shareholders are bussed in from Driftwood. Constance moves the one book that proves everything, the loan ledger she calls the ebb book, to a storm vault on Cape Calamity.

**Act 4 · Launch Window (36 to 40).** The vault's blockhouse is only empty during a real rocket launch, and Upward has scrubbed 31 Saturdays in a row. So the crew makes sure this one flies, on election day, and takes the ledger while the whole county looks up.

**How it ends.** Barbara reads the ledger on air, and then two names. The forced sales are undone, block by block. Royce Bittern does not leave the Cape. Bunny wins by 212 votes and Dale's concession speech is the best-rated thing he ever made. Pelican Rest's residents buy their own common land with the concert money and form an association to hold it. Somebody has to chair it. Everyone looks at Mags.

**What changes in the leads.** Teo learns that an audience is a tool and stops asking it to love him. Mags learns that being left alone was never on offer, and starts choosing what she is in charge of.
