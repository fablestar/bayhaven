# Bible · radio · Swamp Country 94

Swamp Country 94 plays shuffles, train beats and two-steps on banjo, twang guitar and a walking bass. Hoot Gibbons hosts from a porch in the Glades in one flat, friendly drawl. Below: identity, voice, sound recipe, six songs, 20 lines, six idents and two segments.

Tier: A for the station, songs 1 to 3, host lines 1 to 6 and idents 1 and 2. B for songs 4 to 6, host lines 7 to 20, idents 3 to 6 and both segments.

Start rule, the same on every station: a vehicle taken from traffic or the kerb is already tuned to its own station. The first Q or E press sets the lead's remembered station, and garage vehicles open on that.

## The host: Hoot Gibbons

Hoot broadcasts from a porch on a dirt track off Mudcat Road. His dog is called Gravy.

1. He introduces a song by what it is about, never by its title or who plays it.
2. One flat, friendly drawl for everything. A birthday, a hurricane and a shooting get the same voice.
3. He often reports what Gravy is doing. Gravy is never doing much.

Speech blip: saw through an 800 Hz low-pass, 140 Hz, 110 ms per syllable, 4 syllables a second, every syllable slides down 6%.

## Sound recipe

| Part | Value |
|---|---|
| Tempo | 100 to 124 bpm |
| Scales | Major and Mixolydian |
| From the `07` list | kick, snare, hats, guitar-like pluck, banjo |
| New: upright bass | Triangle plus sine at pitch, 5 ms attack, 220 ms decay, 500 Hz low-pass, with a 20 ms noise click at the start of each note |
| New: brush snare | Noise through a 2.5 kHz band-pass, 30 ms attack, 90 ms decay. Used for train beats. |
| New: fiddle | Saw with a 60 ms attack, 5.5 Hz vibrato of 20 cents, 1.8 kHz band-pass |
| New: steel slide | Sine plus triangle whose pitch glides to each new note over 120 ms, volume swelling in over 80 ms |
| Shuffle | `step 8th`, `swing 67%`: 16 steps are two bars. Kick on beats 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, hats on every step, bass walking on the beats. |
| Train | Straight 16ths. Brush snare on every 16th with the accent on each offbeat. Kick on beats 1 and 3. |
| Two-step | Straight. Bass plays root then fifth, kick with the bass, snare on 2 and 4. |

Mix notes: banjo 35% right, guitar 35% left, bass and kick centre. Snares are quiet, −8 dB. The whole station runs through a 90 Hz high-pass and a −3 dB shelf above 7 kHz: one small speaker on a porch. The banjo rolls on every step in verses and choruses: chord root, third, fifth, octave, third, fifth, octave, fifth.

## Reading the songbook

Canon Song format. Drum lines are 16 steps: `x` hit, `X` accent (+3 dB), `.` rest. A step is a 16th note and 16 steps are one bar, unless the first line says `step 8th`; then 16 steps are two bars. `swing 67%` puts every second step two thirds of the way through its pair. Bass is one entry per step, counted from the current chord's root: `1` root, `3` the chord's own third, `5` fifth, `8` octave, other numbers on the major scale, `b` lowers a semitone; `-` holds, `5,` is the fifth below. Lead is one entry per 8th note, two bars, in steps of the song's scale from its key note; `8` is the octave, `6,` is an octave down.

## Host lines

Loop: song, song, host line, ad, ident. Every fourth loop the host line is a segment script. A line is 180 characters or fewer. Each line starts with when it may play. `night` is 20:00 to 05:00, `dawn` 05:00 to 07:00, `storm` the 15:15 rain, `cut` just before the Sheriff's show takes the station at heat 4, `back` the first line after heat clears. Lines 1 to 6 are tier A, the rest B.

1. after-song · That was a song about a truck. Coming up, a song about a different truck.
2. any · This is Swamp Country 94. I'm Hoot Gibbons. That's Gravy on the porch. Gravy's a dog. He's asleep.
3. storm · Well, it's three-fifteen and here's the rain. Gravy went under the porch. It surprises him every day.
4. any · Coming up, a song about a dog. Then one about prison. Then one about a dog waiting outside a prison. That's the range.
5. cut · Sheriff's show is fixing to take the station. Y'all sit tight. I'll be right here. I don't go anywhere.
6. after-ad · I'm told that was an advertisement. I thought it was a short song about a lawyer.
7. back · We're back. Sheriff's show ran long. I'm told two fellas didn't make it. Here's a song about a truck that did.
8. in Mudcat Glades · If you're on Mudcat Road, wave at Big Earl. He won't wave back. He's thirty metres of alligator and he's busy.
9. Friday · Friday. Race night at the Speedbowl at seven. That's cars turning left for two hours. I never tire of it.
10. dawn · Morning. Fog's on the water, frogs are still at it, and Gravy has not moved since yesterday.
11. any · A fella called to ask if we take requests. We do. He asked for a song about a boat. We don't have that.
12. any · That was a slow one. It was about a man shot over a boat trailer. Most of the slow ones are.
13. rain · Rain's coming sideways. Gravy's looking at me like I did it.
14. hurricane · Hurricane's here. I nailed the radio to the porch and the porch to the ground. Here's a song about wind.
15. night · Fireflies are out. So is an airboat, wherever Nilda's going at this hour. I saw nothing. Neither did Gravy.
16. any · They're talking about draining the Glades for houses. Damn shame. I asked Gravy. He said nothing. I'm with Gravy.
17. Sunday · Sunday. Golf tournament up at Mallard Greens. I don't have a song about golf. Nobody does.
18. any · Gus says a gator took a deputy's leg at the boat ramp. Trouser leg. Deputy's fine. Mad as hell, but fine.
19. Monday · Monday. Garbage trucks at dawn. Now that's a truck I don't have a song about. Yet.
20. any · Three chords in that one. We don't use the fourth. It's for emergencies.

## Segments

### Porch Sale (tier B)
Listeners' things for sale or trade. The number is always 555-0161.

1. "Porch Sale. A fella on Mudcat Road has a Mullet pickup, runs good, no doors. He'll trade for doors. Any doors. Call 555-0161 and let it ring. He's usually out back."
2. "Porch Sale. Fella's selling a boat with two holes in it. He says they're for drainage. They're about the size of a pistol round. Comes with a shotgun, fired once, in error. No questions. 555-0161."
3. "Porch Sale. A lady at the fish camp has forty lawn flamingos she didn't order. They turned up overnight. She'd like them gone before the association counts them. Free. 555-0161."

### Gator Watch (tier B)
Gus Thibodeaux of Big Earl's phones in the day's alligators that are where they shouldn't be. Hoot reads the list. 

1. "Gator Watch, from Gus at Big Earl's. One in a swimming pool on Coot Circle. One on the ninth green, lying across the hole. One in a drive-through lane on Gateway Boulevard. He hadn't ordered."
2. "Gator Watch. Gus says the big fella in Lake Serenity got a letter from the association. He ate the letter. Then, Gus reckons, the man who brought it. Nobody's seen him since Tuesday."
3. "Gator Watch. Quiet day. One gator under a truck at the Bait & Ballistics. Gus says leave him be. It's his truck now. That's how that works."
