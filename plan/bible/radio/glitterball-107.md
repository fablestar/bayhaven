# Bible · radio · Glitterball 107

Glitterball 107 plays disco and house: a kick on every beat, octave basses, string swoops and piano stabs. Mirrorball Mona calls every listener darling and pays a compliment before she says anything else. Below: identity, voice, sound recipe, six songs, 20 lines, six idents and two segments.

Tier: C for everything in this file.

Start rule, the same on every station: a vehicle taken from traffic or the kerb is already tuned to its own station. The first Q or E press sets the lead's remembered station, and garage vehicles open on that.

## The host: Mirrorball Mona

Mona ran the door at Club Luma for twenty years and never turned away anyone who could dance, or anyone who tried. She has never left a party first.

1. Every listener is "darling". Once in a line, never twice.
2. A compliment comes first, then the news, even when the news is a hurricane or a shooting. She swears the way she dresses: rarely, and on purpose.
3. She describes the world as light: the bay is sequins, the storm is a strobe, the Sheriff's helicopter is a follow spot.

Speech blip: sine plus a quiet saw, 300 Hz, every syllable glides up 3 semitones, 85 ms per syllable, 5 syllables a second, with a 200 ms shimmer of reverb.

Mix notes: kick 0 dB, bass −3 and ducking 4 dB under each kick, piano −6, strings −8, hats −9. Before every chorus or drop, a low-pass on the whole music bus opens from 400 Hz to fully open over the last 4 bars. Songs are long: three to four minutes each.

## Reading the songbook

Canon Song format. Drum lines are 16 steps, one bar of 16ths: `x` hit, `o` open hat, `.` rest. `swing 54%` puts every second step 54% of the way through its pair. Bass is one entry per step, counted from the current chord's root: `1` root, `5` fifth, `8` octave. Lead is one entry per 8th note, two bars, in steps of the song's scale from its key note; `8` is the octave. Patterns loop under the whole chord loop.

## Segments

### Mona's Mirror
A caller says what they are wearing. Mona approves, whatever it is, and adds one note.

1. "Mona's Mirror. Our caller has somebody else's blood on a white suit. Darling, cold water, club soda and a better class of friend. Red is your colour. Don't wear it again."
2. "Mona's Mirror. A deputy, on duty, in the teal with the pink stripe. Darling, that uniform has always been fabulous. It's the best thing about the department. Wear it like you chose it."
3. "Mona's Mirror. A gentleman in Mallard Greens asks if blush counts as a colour. Darling, there are four approved colours up there and you found the best one. Add a scarf. They haven't banned scarves yet."

### Mona's List
Mona reads who is on the list tonight. Everybody is. `{vehicle}` and `{street}` are the player's own at that moment; with the player on foot, use script 2 or 3.

1. "Mona's List. On the list tonight: the night shift at Bayhaven General, table three at Domino Park, and whoever is driving that {vehicle} on {street}. Yes, you, darling. Plus one."
2. "Mona's List. On the list tonight: every valet at Hotel Alba, the lady with the sandwich truck, and Gerald, who is a pelican and has never once paid. Not on the list: nobody. That is the point of the list."
3. "Mona's List. On the list: the Dawn Patrol, all of them, in trainers. The lifeguards. The man in the flamingo float, if he makes it back. Darling, if you can hear this, you're on it."
